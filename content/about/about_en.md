# About gitmgf

gitmgf is a community where clinicians — and anyone building tools for clinicians — share, discover and improve together the digital tools they make: calculators, web and desktop apps, spreadsheets, scripts, browser extensions and AI prompts. The focus is **primary care (General Practice / Family Medicine)** and **residency**, without excluding hospital care.

We believe the practical knowledge each colleague turns into a tool should travel: what solves a problem in one practice can solve it in many others.

## What drives us

- **Sharing.** A useful tool shouldn't stay stuck on one computer or in one messaging group. Posting its link on gitmgf is the simplest way to put it within reach of every colleague.
- **Collaboration.** Anyone who uses a tool can star it, suggest improvements and request features directly from its author. We prefer open source because it lets others adapt and fix a tool and give their improvements back to the community.
- **Community.** Residents, supervisors and specialists learn from one another. Seeing what colleagues build sparks new ideas and saves everyone from starting from scratch.
- **Recognition.** Every tool has a named author with a public profile. People who spend their time building for others deserve visibility and credit.

## How it started

gitmgf grew out of the **"Inteligência Artificial e Literacia Digital"** (Artificial Intelligence and Digital Literacy) training, which we organised with the **Coordination of the Family Medicine Residency Programme of Lisboa e Vale do Tejo** (Coordenação do Internato Médico de Medicina Geral e Familiar de Lisboa e Vale do Tejo). It started in 2026 for **Family Medicine residents**, and also includes a **course for residency supervisors** (orientadores de formação).

During the sessions, residents and supervisors showed us tools they had built for everyday consultations and residency work — and we felt there should be a place where they could share them with their colleagues. gitmgf is the answer: one place, in Portuguese, to share them, find them and improve them together.

## Why

Many professionals build useful tools — indicator templates, residency portfolio helpers, scores, export scripts — that end up scattered across messaging groups, personal repositories and links passed around by word of mouth. People who need them can't find them; people who made them get no visibility or credit; and colleagues end up each building, on their own, tools that already exist.

Bringing these tools together in one place helps people who need them find them, gives credit to the people who made them, and makes room for colleagues to collaborate instead of duplicating work.

## How it works

1. **Publish.** Anyone can create an account and submit a listing with a description, licence, data handling and links to where the tool is published.
2. **Review.** Every listing is visible as soon as it is submitted, marked “Awaiting validation”, and a moderator reviews it. Review checks the [Submission Guidelines](/en/p/guidelines), not clinical correctness.
3. **Discover.** Search works in Portuguese and English, with or without accents. Ranking blends text relevance with real use: clicks from search, outbound clicks, stars and GitHub activity, with more weight on recent use.
4. **Collaborate.** Anyone who uses a tool can star it and send suggestions or feature requests to its author, who receives them by email and in their notifications. For open-source tools, anyone can also contribute directly in the repository.

## How to take part

- **Share** a tool you built, even if it seems simple: many of the most useful tools are small.
- **Try** your colleagues' tools and star the ones you find useful.
- **Suggest improvements** to authors and, if a tool is open source, contribute in its repository.
- **Spread the word** about gitmgf among colleagues in your practice and residency programme.

gitmgf is **only a directory**: it does not host or run tools. There are no ads or paid placements, and pages use no analytics cookies or third-party services. Read more in the [Privacy Policy](/en/p/privacy).
