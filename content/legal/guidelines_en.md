# Submission Guidelines

**Version 2026-11 · Last updated: 16 November 2026**

These guidelines complement the [Terms of Use](/en/p/terms) and explain what can be published on gitmgf and how to prepare a listing that passes review the first time. Every listing is visible as soon as it is submitted, marked “Awaiting validation”, and is reviewed by a moderator; if it is not approved, it is taken down.

## 1. What we accept

Digital tools that are useful for clinical practice, health management and teaching, focused on primary care and residency, without excluding hospital care:

- web apps, online calculators, desktop and mobile apps;
- spreadsheets and templates;
- scripts, libraries and browser extensions;
- AI prompts and assistants;
- aggregated, anonymised datasets.

Tools can be free or paid, open source or proprietary. We prefer open source, but it is not required.

## 2. What we don't accept

- Tools unrelated to health, advertising or spam.
- Tools that don't work, broken links or "under construction" pages.
- Content that infringes third-party copyright or licences.
- Tools that collect data without telling users, malware or obfuscated code.
- Duplicates of an existing listing (edit the existing one if it is yours).

## 3. Patient data: an absolute rule

**Never** include data that identifies patients, directly or indirectly, in the text, screenshots or examples: names, patient numbers, dates of birth, addresses, photos, records or screens of clinical systems with real data. Use fictional data and check images before uploading them.

Say truthfully how the tool handles patient data: not at all, only on the user's device, or sent to a server. If it sends data to a server, we recommend declaring GDPR compliance (see section 6) — without it, the moderator may request changes.

## 4. Links

- Links must point to **where the author publishes the tool**: repository (GitHub, GitLab, Codeberg), own website, releases page or app store.
- Don't use file-sharing services, download mirrors or URL shorteners.
- Downloads must come from the author's releases page.
- Only `http://` or `https://` addresses are accepted.

gitmgf does not host, run or distribute tools: it only keeps the listing and the links.

## 5. A good listing

- A clear **title** (up to 80 characters) and a one-sentence **summary** (up to 200).
- A **description** of what it does, who it is for, how to use it and its known limitations.
- **Intended use**: one to three sentences on who should use the tool, for what and with which limitations.
- **Categories** (1 to 5) and **tags** that help people find it.
- Correct **licence**, **pricing**, **platforms** and **languages**.
- **Screenshots** with alternative text that describes the image.
- An English version is optional but helps colleagues in other countries.

## 6. Certifications and compliance

By default, no certification is declared. Switch on only what the tool really has and give the evidence:

- **CE marking / MDR / IVDR**: legal basis, manufacturer, class, notified body number (4 digits) and certificate number when the class requires them, validity date, and a link to the EU Declaration of Conformity or certificate.
- **GDPR (self-declared)**: a link to the privacy policy and where personal data is stored.
- **ISO standards, IEC 62304, EU AI Act, accessibility and others**: the evidence the form asks for.

The moderator checks that the evidence opens and matches the tool, manufacturer and class. A claim without valid evidence is rejected; the rest of the listing can still be published. False or misleading claims breach the Terms.

## 7. Review

Among other points, the moderator checks that:

1. links work and point to what is described;
2. there is no patient data in text or images;
3. intended use and data handling are plausible;
4. the licence matches the repository;
5. links point to the author's own publication place;
6. there is no advertising or spam;
7. every certification has evidence that matches the tool.

gitmgf **does not validate clinical correctness**. Review checks compliance with these guidelines, not scientific accuracy.

If a listing is rejected or changes are requested, you receive an email with the reason. You can fix it and submit again, or appeal the decision (see Terms, section 8).
